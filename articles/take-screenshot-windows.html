How to Take a Screenshot on Windows

Updated October 26, 2026 · 4 min read · TechFixDesk Editorial Team

Windows includes several ways to capture your screen. Choose the method that fits whether you need a quick copy, a saved image, or part of the display.

🛠️
Before you begin: Save your work and back up important data before making system-level changes. Use official software sources and stop if a step does not match your device.

1. Capture a selected area

Press Windows + Shift + S to open the snipping overlay on supported Windows versions. Choose a capture mode.

2. Capture the full screen to a file

Press Windows + PrtScn on supported devices to save a screenshot automatically. Look in Pictures → Screenshots. Some laptops require Fn.

3. Copy a window

Use Alt + PrtScn on supported setups to copy the active window, then paste into Paint, a document, or a chat.

4. Share safely

Check screenshots for passwords, private messages, account numbers, or other sensitive information before sharing.

Key takeaway: Review screenshots for private information before posting them publicly.
